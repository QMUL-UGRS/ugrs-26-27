date: 2026-04-08

room: "Mathematical Sciences Building, MB-203 (or via Teams)"
abstract: >
  What shapes can a two-dimensional universe have? If we lived on the
  surface of a world with no "outside," what possibilities would there be?
  This talk explores one of the most beautiful results in topology: the
  complete classification of surfaces. Starting from simple operations like
  cutting and glueing polygons, it builds up to every possible 2-dimensional
  world, introducing along the way the key idea that topology studies shape
  up to continuous deformation — allowing us to understand when the shape of
  two worlds is fundamentally the same. No prior knowledge of topology is
  required; only curiosity about shape, symmetry, and the hidden structure
  behind familiar surfaces like the torus and the Klein bottle.
