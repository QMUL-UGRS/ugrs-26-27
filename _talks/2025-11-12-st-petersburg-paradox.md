title: "The St. Petersburg Paradox — Links to Probability Theory and Applications in Finance"
speaker: "Eve Mladenova"
date: 2025-11-12
time: "15:00–16:00"
room: "Mathematical Sciences Building, MB-204 (or via Teams)"
abstract: >
  A look at the famous 18th-century St. Petersburg paradox, and how it
  connects to real problems in modern finance. What starts as a simple
  coin-toss game turns out to explain why some financial models break down
  when rare, extreme events — so-called "black swans" — dominate outcomes.
  This talk looks at how these heavy-tailed risks affect trading, and why
  strategies that seem profitable can actually lose money in the long run,
  using ideas from probability such as the Law of Large Numbers, the Central
  Limit Theorem, and expected utility to understand how mathematicians and
  traders deal with uncertainty in the real world.
tags: [probability, finance]
