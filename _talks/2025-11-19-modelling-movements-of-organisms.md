title: "Modelling the Movements of Organisms"
speaker: "Norberto Lucero-Azuara"
date: 2025-11-19
time: "15:00–16:00"
room: "Mathematical Sciences Building, MB-204 (or via Teams)"
abstract: >
  A century ago, Sir Ronald Ross and Karl Pearson pioneered the use of random
  walks to model organism migration. This stochastic framework was later
  refined through the Langevin equation, which captures the Brownian motion
  of a tracer particle suspended in a fluid. Unlike passive tracers, however,
  biological agents are self-propelled, giving rise to the modern concept of
  active particles — and, in parallel, advanced stochastic models were
  developed to describe anomalous diffusion, deviations from classical
  Brownian motion observed in the movement of living organisms. This talk
  introduces the three research domains behind this framework — movement
  ecology, active matter, and anomalous diffusion — reviews studies deriving
  generalised Langevin equations from statistical analyses of bumblebee
  flight data, and presents models of Active Brownian Particles extended to
  the corresponding generalised Langevin equation in the comoving frame.
