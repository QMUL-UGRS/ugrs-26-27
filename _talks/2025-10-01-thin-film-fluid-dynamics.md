abstract: >
  What do avalanches, our lungs, air hockey, bouncing balls, pond skaters and
  heavy industrial machinery all have in common? They are all examples of a
  phenomenon in fluid dynamics called "thin film flows". Tiny thin layers of
  fluid are able to generate massive forces under the right conditions, and
  over time we have been able to study these flows and generate equations
  that model their motion and behaviour. This talk derives the governing
  equations of lubrication theory and thin film flows from first principles,
  applies them to a real-life scenario, and discusses some further
  applications. Some knowledge of vector calculus is helpful to follow along
  but not essential — the results are much more interesting than the details.
