abstract: >
  The Double Bubble problem asks which configuration or configurations of two
  sets with given volumes minimize their surface area — or boundary, in the
  two-dimensional case. The solution naturally depends on the measure used to
  calculate surface area or boundary. This talk considers the two-dimensional
  Double Bubble problem in which boundary is measured with respect to the
  ℓ₁-norm, and shows how simple ideas, simple techniques, and a touch of
  ancient mythology can still be used to solve problems at the level of
  doctoral research.
