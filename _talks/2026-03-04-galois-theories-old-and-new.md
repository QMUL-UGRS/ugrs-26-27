abstract: >
  Galois famously developed his theory of permutation groups of roots of
  polynomial equations in 1830, to understand the long-standing problem of
  solvability of equations by radicals. An analogous theory of symmetries of
  linear differential equations was initiated in 1883 by Picard and Vessiot.
  The theory of the fundamental group of topological spaces can be viewed as
  a geometric analogue of Galois theory, and Grothendieck's 1970s theory of
  the étale fundamental group is a direct algebraic-geometric
  generalisation of it. In all these instances, a category of objects of
  interest is classified by actions of a certain type of group, and any
  result of this type can be considered a form of Galois theory. Janelidze
  developed a very general categorical Galois theory in the 1980s; this talk
  shows how it broadly encompasses all of the above examples, and discusses
  recent work that vastly generalises differential Galois theory.
