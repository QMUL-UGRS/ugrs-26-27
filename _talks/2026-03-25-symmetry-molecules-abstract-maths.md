title: "Symmetry, Molecules, and the Power of Abstract Maths"

date: 2026-03-25
time: "16:00–17:00"
room: "Mathematical Sciences Building, MB-203 (or via Teams)"
abstract: >
  Mathematicians sometimes study very abstract ideas without worrying too
  much about where they might be useful — group theory is a classic example.
  Yet this abstract theory turns out to be enormously useful in chemistry.
  This talk looks at how the symmetries of molecules can be described using
  groups, and how this allows chemists to extract real physical information,
  seeing along the way how symmetry helps predict spectroscopic transitions
  and can greatly simplify quantum chemistry calculations by breaking large
  problems down into smaller ones. The goal is not to dive deeply into
  formal mathematics, but to show how a surprisingly abstract idea becomes a
  practical tool for understanding molecules.
