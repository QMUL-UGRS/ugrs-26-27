abstract: >
  A deceptively simple question: what shape do soap films take? Focusing
  primarily on films formed by dipping a fixed wire frame in water, this
  talk introduces the Plateau problem, which asks what area-minimising
  surfaces with a prescribed boundary look like. After motivating a
  pointwise condition for such shapes, it delves into the existence,
  uniqueness, and regularity of solutions, then shifts to less regular
  boundaries, such as the edges of a tetrahedron, observing Plateau's laws
  as they appear in these complex cases — concluding by exploring why
  free-floating soap bubbles form spheres, linking this natural phenomenon
  to Dido's problem and the mathematics of an ancient Greek legend.
